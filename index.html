About this map

Every pin is a Vienna address with one or more people or businesses listed in the alphabetical residents' directory (Band 1) of Lehmann's Wohnungsanzeiger for 1938 — Vienna's address book on the eve of World War II. Of 490,781 entries, 127,308 could be matched to a street address and placed on the modern map as 30,082 pins.

How it was built

The 1,644-page scan was rendered page by page, split into columns, and OCR'd (Tesseract with a Fraktur model). A parsing pipeline extracted names, occupations and addresses, stripped page headers, footers and advertisements, and normalized historical street abbreviations. Each address was then joined against the City of Vienna's official address registry (“Adressen Standorte Wien”, CC BY 4.0). Exact matches are blue, registry matches with a number fallback are purple, fuzzy street matches are amber.

The AI part

This pipeline was built by Muse by Meta, an AI research assistant, working with Moshe Berman. The agent wrote the OCR, parsing and geocoding code, ran the batches, validated samples against the page scans, and built this site. Every stage reported measured numbers — match rates, not adjectives — and uncertain matches are flagged on the map rather than hidden.

Caveats

OCR misreads names; some 1938 streets were renamed or no longer exist; amber pins are approximate. Absence of a pin doesn't mean absence from the book — only 48.7% of addressed entries matched the modern registry.
