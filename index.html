Every pin is a Vienna address with one or more people or businesses listed in Lehmann's Wohnungsanzeiger, Vienna's address book. The year picker switches between full layers for 1938–1942. Each record popup shows the scanned row it came from.

Build log

  1. Scans of the Lehmann volumes from the Wienbibliothek's Retrodigitalisierung copies (public domain).
  2. The library's per-page Transkribus ALTO OCR, with bounding boxes for every text line.
  3. Parsing names, occupations, addresses and unit numbers (Stg./Tür/Top); headers, footers and ads stripped.
  4. Street-name normalization — ambiguous forms held for review, not silently fixed.
  5. Geocoding against the City of Vienna address registry (Adressen Standorte Wien, CC BY 4.0).
  6. Tiered pins: blue exact, purple registry, amber fuzzy-approximate.
  7. Full 1938–1942 layers.
  8. Scanned-row snippets in every record popup.

Caveats

OCR misreads names; some streets were renamed or no longer exist; amber pins are approximate. A missing pin doesn't mean the entry is missing from the book — not every address matched the modern registry.

Method

The library's OCR of the page scans is parsed into names, occupations and addresses; street names are normalized (ambiguous forms held for review); each address is matched against the City of Vienna's address registry (Adressen Standorte Wien, CC BY 4.0), and uncertain matches are flagged on the map rather than hidden. The pipeline was built by Muse by Meta working with Moshe Berman, and every stage reported measured match rates.
